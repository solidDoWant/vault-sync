`seed-100055-retention.json` and `seed-100062.json` are seeds 100055 and 100062 of the nightly run (3000 steps, 3 clients, 2 engines; #485). The first is the nightly's minimized retention case, minimized again to 42 steps with a matcher on `phantom-delete`, and the second is the nightly's minimized plain case (1500 steps). In both, the user renamed a synced note (`trailing.` to `notes/c.md`; `NOTES (conflict c0 1)` to `straße.md`) and the app was killed before the engine recorded the rename. The full scan after the restart pushed a delete of the note plus a create of the renamed file, as #167 item 2's known limitation does. The server refused the create because the path was taken (by the note `NOTES` that came back on a case-insensitive platform, or by another note at `straße.md`), so the engine kept the file at its conflict copy (`notes (conflict e4 …)/c.md`, `straße (conflict e4 …).md`) under the new document, as it would have kept a refused rename. The `phantom-delete` check excused a lost rename's tombstone only for a create at a path the user's renames gave the file, so it reported these. It now also accepts a create at the engine's own conflict copy of such a path. The engine is unchanged. The `phantom-delete` of seeds 100071 and 100083 (retention) follows the same pattern (`notes (conflict e3 …)/c.md`, `d (conflict e4 …).md`), and that of seed 100087 (retention) no longer shows either; they are not committed.
